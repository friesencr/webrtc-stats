WebRTCStats loopback demo

Two RTCPeerConnections in one tab exchange an offer/answer over loopback ICE. One camera/mic capture is shared to both peers so a single device works reliably. Charts use parsed stats when the browser exposes them (names vary by engine). Core: bitrate, jitter, RTT, loss. Advanced: video drops/freezes, NACK/retransmits, encoder quality limitation (Chrome), audio concealment samples. Use New session to tear down WebRTCStats and create a fresh instance (same as users who end a call and start another without refreshing the page). Use Share screen to exercise wrapGetDisplayMedia: the library emits getDisplayMedia timeline events (request/stream/error) just like it does for getUserMedia. The screen replaces only peer 1's outbound video (via replaceTrack), so expect peer 2's inbound bitrate to shift to screen-share levels while peer 1's inbound stays on camera (peer 2 is still sending its camera track unchanged) — that asymmetry is the intended behavior, not a bug.

These plots matter because they reveal smoothness (drops/freezes), loss recovery (NACK/retransmits), why the encoder is backing off (quality limitation), and audio gap-filling (concealment)—issues that bitrate and delay alone often miss. qualityLimitationReason is ordinal: 0 none, 1 cpu, 2 bandwidth, 3 other, 4 unknown. Freeze/drop/concealment are cumulative counters from inbound reports.
